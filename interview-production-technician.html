The last checkpoint before it ships.

Every pack that reaches a customer has already run a full cycle on our own test rigs. We sat down with one of our production technicians to talk through what that verification actually involves.

What does end-of-line testing actually check?

Every pack runs a full charge and discharge cycle under load, while we log voltage, temperature and current across every cell. We're checking that the pack performs the way the design promised, not just that it powers on.

How does a pack get flagged before it even reaches the rig?

Visual and mechanical inspection first — connectors seated properly, wiring routed the way the drawing shows, nothing loose or out of place. A lot of problems are easier to catch by hand than by instrument.

How long does a full test cycle take?

It depends on the pack's capacity and the application's duty cycle, but a complete charge and discharge verification is measured in hours, not minutes. We'd rather a pack sit on the rig longer than skip a step.

What happens when a pack doesn't pass?

It doesn't leave the building. It goes back to whoever built that stage of the pack, with the exact reading that failed, so the issue gets fixed at the source rather than patched afterward.

What tells you a pack is actually ready to ship?

Every number on the test log matches what the design promised, with nothing hand-waved or "close enough." That log travels with the pack — it's the proof that what shipped is exactly what was tested.
