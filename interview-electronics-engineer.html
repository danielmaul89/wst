Inside the electronics team.

Every pack that leaves WS Technicals carries a battery management system built and proven in-house. We sat down with one of our electronics engineers to talk through what that work actually looks like, week to week.

What does a battery management system actually have to do?

It has to know the exact state of every cell in the pack, all the time, and act on that faster than the application can get itself into trouble. Voltage, temperature, current, balance between cells — the BMS is the thing that keeps all of that inside safe limits, and reports it honestly to whatever the pack is powering.

Where does a project like that start?

With the application, not the electronics. A drone pack and a warehouse robot pack have completely different duty cycles, so the first real work is understanding how the machine is actually going to use the battery — peak draw, ambient temperature range, how it charges, how it fails safely if something goes wrong. The schematic comes after that, not before.

How much of the work is at a desk versus at a bench?

More bench than people expect. A schematic can look correct and still behave differently once it is soldered, powered and put under real load. So a lot of the week is probing boards, comparing what the multimeter shows against what the simulation predicted, and figuring out why they disagree when they do.

What's the hardest part of the job to get right?

Edge cases. The system has to behave correctly not just when everything is normal, but when a sensor fails, a connector is loose, or the pack is charged in weather it was never meant to see. Most of the engineering time goes into those situations, not the happy path.

What tells you a design is actually ready?

When it survives the tests we designed specifically to break it, not just the ones it was obviously going to pass. Every finished pack still goes through a full charge and discharge cycle on the production floor before it ships — that's the last checkpoint, and it doesn't get skipped.
